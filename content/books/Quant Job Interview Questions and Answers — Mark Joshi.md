**Interview-prep volume weighted toward derivatives pricing, stochastic calculus, and C++.**

## Why Read This

Where [[A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews — Xinfeng Zhou|Zhou]] is broadly applicable across quant trader and quant researcher roles, Joshi is the right book if you are targeting derivatives-pricing or quant-developer positions at investment banks. Heavier on Black-Scholes mechanics, risk-neutral pricing intuition, numerical methods (Monte Carlo, finite differences, trees), and C++ implementation questions.

## Key Takeaways

- Risk-neutral pricing questions are best answered by knowing two or three derivations cold and being able to recognize when an interviewer's question is asking for one of them — see [[Black-Scholes Equation]], [[Feynman-Kac Theorem]].
- Monte Carlo, PDE methods, and binomial trees give different answers under different conditions; Joshi drills the cases where each is appropriate.
- C++ questions in the book are not about language trivia — they test whether you can write performant numerical code (memory layout, virtual dispatch costs, RAII).
- Several questions probe [[Greeks]] and Greek hedging intuition past the textbook definitions.
