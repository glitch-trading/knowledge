**Narrative history of the quant industry through the lens of the 2007 quant crisis.**

## Why Read This

Useful as cultural and historical context for the field you're trying to enter. Patterson follows Cliff Asness (AQR), Boaz Weinstein (Deutsche Bank / Saba), Peter Muller (PDT), Ken Griffin ([[The Man Who Solved the Market — Gregory Zuckerman|Citadel]]), and Jim Simons (Renaissance) into the August 2007 quant meltdown — when nearly every statistical arbitrage fund blew up in the same week because they were unwittingly running the same factor exposures.

The lesson is recurring: crowded trades, hidden correlations, and leverage turn separately-managed risk into shared systemic risk.

## Key Takeaways

- The 2007 quant crisis was a deleveraging cascade, not a model failure. Funds with sound individual strategies hit drawdowns because *other* funds in the same trades were forced to unwind.
- "Diversified across many independent strategies" can be a lie when every strategy loads on the same hidden factor.
- [[Position Sizing]] under leverage is the difference between a bad month and a fund-ending event.
- Renaissance was structurally different from peers (closed to outside money, extreme secrecy, integrated team) and survived 2007 without significant damage.
- The book usefully names firms, eras, and personalities — useful priors before reading more technical industry material like [[Inside the Black Box — Rishi Narang]].
